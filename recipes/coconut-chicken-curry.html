Easy Coconut Chicken Curry – Ready in 35 Minutes

★★★★★ 4.9 (267 ratings)

This coconut chicken curry is everything you want on a weeknight — rich, warmly spiced, and absolutely packed with flavor, all from one pan in just 35 minutes. Tender chicken thighs simmer in a creamy, fragrant coconut milk sauce that tastes like you spent hours in the kitchen, even though you absolutely did not.

I grew up eating curry at a little Thai spot near our apartment, and I spent years trying to recreate that flavor at home. The secret? It's not some rare ingredient you have to hunt down — it's all about how you treat the curry paste. Once I figured that out, this became the most-requested dinner in our house. My husband asks for it at least once a week, and my kids who once claimed they "don't like spicy food" now go back for seconds. That tells you everything you need to know.

About the Key Ingredients

Everything in this recipe is available at a standard US supermarket — Kroger, Whole Foods, Walmart, Trader Joe's. Here's what you're working with and where to find anything unfamiliar:

  • Red curry paste — this is the backbone of the dish. Find it in the Asian foods aisle of any major US grocery store. Maesri and Thai Kitchen are the most common brands on shelves. Thai Kitchen is milder and works great if you're cooking for kids or spice-sensitive folks. Maesri has a bolder, more complex flavor.
  • Full-fat coconut milk — please use full-fat, not light. The richness of the fat is what gives the sauce that luxurious body. Thai Kitchen, Chaokoh, and store brands all work well. Shake the can before opening.
  • Fish sauce — don't let it intimidate you. It's at Whole Foods, Kroger, most Asian grocery stores, and increasingly at Walmart in the Asian foods aisle. Red Boat and Tiparos are popular brands. It smells pungent in the bottle but mellows completely when cooked, adding a deep savory richness you can't get any other way. Don't skip it. If you absolutely must, soy sauce is the substitute, but the depth won't be quite the same.
  • Coconut oil — for searing and cooking. It pairs beautifully with the curry flavors. Substitute vegetable oil if you don't have it.
  • Fresh ginger — sold in the produce section near garlic and onions. A knob of ginger lasts weeks in the fridge. Grate it fresh — it's a world apart from dried ginger powder.

One Key Technique: Blooming the Curry Paste

The single most important thing you'll do in this recipe is add the red curry paste to the pan and cook it for a full minute before adding any liquid. This is called "blooming" — and it's the difference between a curry that tastes flat and a curry that tastes like it came from a Thai restaurant. The heat activates and deepens the fat-soluble compounds in the paste, releasing their full aromatic potential. You'll know it's working when the paste turns slightly darker and your kitchen smells incredible. Don't rush this step, and don't add liquid too soon.

Easy Coconut Chicken Curry

Tender chicken thighs in a rich, creamy coconut milk and red curry sauce — one pan, 35 minutes, better than takeout.

Prep 10 min
Cook 25 min
Total 35 min
Serves 4
Calories 490 kcal
📌 Save to Pinterest

Ingredients

4 servings

Instructions

  1. 1

    Sear the chicken. Heat coconut oil in a large skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high heat until shimmering. Add chicken thighs and sear 4–5 minutes per side until deep golden brown on each side. The chicken doesn't need to be fully cooked through yet — it'll finish in the sauce. Remove to a plate and set aside.

  2. 2

    Build the aromatics. In the same pan, add diced onion and cook over medium heat for 3 minutes until softened and translucent. Add minced garlic and grated ginger and cook for 1 minute until fragrant. Add the red curry paste and stir constantly for a full minute — you want it to sizzle, darken slightly, and coat everything. This step is what makes the curry taste deep and complex.

  3. 3

    Add the coconut milk. Pour in the full-fat coconut milk and chicken broth, stirring to fully incorporate the curry paste into the liquid. Add the fish sauce and sugar. Stir well and bring to a gentle simmer.

  4. 4

    Simmer with the chicken and vegetables. Return the seared chicken and any resting juices to the pan. Simmer uncovered for 10 minutes. In the last 5 minutes, add the sliced red bell pepper. In the very last minute, stir in the baby spinach and let it wilt into the sauce.

  5. 5

    Finish and serve. Remove from heat. Squeeze fresh lime juice over the curry and taste — adjust salt or add more fish sauce as needed. Garnish generously with fresh cilantro (or parsley). Serve immediately over steamed jasmine rice or with warm naan bread.

Pro Tips for Perfect Coconut Chicken Curry

  • Don't skip the curry paste bloom. Cooking the paste in oil for a full minute before adding liquid is the single biggest flavor upgrade in this recipe. Resist the urge to rush it.
  • Use full-fat coconut milk. Light coconut milk will give you a thin, watery curry. The fat in full-fat coconut milk is what creates that creamy, restaurant-style sauce.
  • Where to find red curry paste: It's in the Asian foods aisle of virtually every US grocery store — Kroger, Walmart, Whole Foods, Target. Maesri and Thai Kitchen are common brands. Thai Kitchen tends to be milder.
  • Fish sauce at US stores: Look for it in the Asian foods aisle at Whole Foods, Kroger, most Walmarts, and any Asian grocery store. Red Boat and Tiparos are reliable brands.
  • Adjust heat to taste. Start with 1 tablespoon of red curry paste if cooking for spice-sensitive eaters, up to 3 tablespoons if you like it fiery.

Variations to Try

  • Shrimp coconut curry: Swap chicken for 1 lb large shrimp. Add shrimp in the last 3–4 minutes of cooking — they cook very fast and will turn rubbery if overcooked.
  • Vegetarian version: Replace chicken with 1 can drained chickpeas and 1 cup cubed sweet potato. Add sweet potato with the coconut milk and chickpeas in the last 5 minutes.
  • Green curry: Use green curry paste instead of red for a brighter, slightly different flavor profile. Reduce to 1.5 tablespoons as green paste can be spicier.
  • Add bamboo shoots: A drained can of bamboo shoots stirred in with the broth adds an authentic Thai restaurant texture.
  • Peanut butter twist: Stir 1 tablespoon of natural peanut butter into the sauce for extra richness and nutty depth.

Serving Suggestions

Coconut chicken curry is incredibly versatile. Here's how to make it a full meal:

  • Steamed jasmine rice — the classic pairing, soaks up the sauce beautifully
  • Warm naan bread — for scooping and mopping up every drop of sauce
  • Cauliflower rice — for a lower-carb option that still works wonderfully
  • Rice noodles — for a Thai-noodle-style bowl
  • A simple cucumber salad on the side to cool things down
  • Extra fresh lime wedges on the table — a squeeze brightens every bite

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 4 days. The curry actually tastes better the next day as flavors deepen overnight.

Freezer

Freeze for up to 3 months in a freezer-safe container. Coconut milk curries freeze surprisingly well. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before reheating.

Reheat

Warm gently in a skillet over medium-low heat, adding a splash of coconut milk or water to loosen the sauce. Stir gently to recombine. Microwave works in a pinch — cover and heat in 1-minute intervals.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is red curry paste?

Red curry paste is a concentrated blend of red chilies, lemongrass, galangal, garlic, shallots, kaffir lime zest, and shrimp paste (some brands are vegan — check the label). It's the flavor foundation of Thai red curry. You'll find it in the Asian foods aisle of most US grocery stores. Thai Kitchen is the most widely available brand and is milder in heat. Maesri is more authentic and bolder in flavor. Both work great in this recipe.

Can I use light coconut milk?

Technically yes, but I really recommend against it. Light coconut milk has significantly less fat, which means your sauce will be thin and watery rather than rich and creamy. If you're watching calories, the better trade-off is to use full-fat coconut milk and eat a slightly smaller serving — you'll be far more satisfied by the flavor and texture. Full-fat coconut milk is also more filling, so you tend to eat less overall.

Is this curry spicy?

With 2 tablespoons of Thai Kitchen red curry paste, this curry is mild to medium — there's warmth but it won't set your mouth on fire. Use 1 tablespoon for a very mild curry, 3 tablespoons if you want more heat. The coconut milk also naturally tones down the spice level. Maesri curry paste is spicier than Thai Kitchen, so use slightly less if switching brands.

Where can I find fish sauce in US stores?

Fish sauce is much easier to find than most people expect! Look in the Asian foods aisle at Whole Foods, Kroger, Publix, most Walmarts, and Target stores. Any Asian grocery store will definitely carry it. Popular brands include Red Boat (premium, worth it), Tiparos, and Thai Kitchen. If you truly cannot find it, soy sauce is the substitute — use 1 tablespoon soy sauce in place of 1 tablespoon fish sauce. The flavor is different but still great.
