Homemade Cottage Cheese – Easy Step-by-Step Recipe

By Mars Published July 5, 2026
★★★★★ 4.9 (104 ratings)
📌 Save to Pinterest Share

Making homemade cottage cheese is one of those kitchen projects that sounds complicated but is actually surprisingly simple. With just milk, an acid, and salt, you can produce fresh, creamy cottage cheese that tastes noticeably better than most store-bought versions — and you’ll know exactly what went into it.

There are several compelling reasons to make cottage cheese at home. First, the ingredient list is as clean as it gets: milk, vinegar or lemon juice, and salt. No stabilizers, no preservatives, no thickeners with names you can’t pronounce. Second, the cost savings are significant — a gallon of milk produces roughly two cups of cottage cheese, often at half the price of premium brands. Third, you can customize the texture, saltiness, and fat content to your exact preferences.

Homemade Cottage Cheese

Learn how to make homemade cottage cheese from scratch with just milk, vinegar, and salt — no rennet needed. Ready in under an hour.

Prep 10 min
Cook 40 min
Total 50 min
Serves 6
Calories 220 kcal
📌 Save to Pinterest

Ingredients

6 servings

Instructions

  1. 1

    Pour the milk into a large heavy-bottomed pot and heat over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until it reaches 120°F (49°C).

  2. 2

    Remove from heat and slowly stir in the vinegar or lemon juice for about 30 seconds until the milk separates into curds and yellowish whey.

  3. 3

    Cover and let sit undisturbed for 15-30 minutes at room temperature so the curds fully form.

  4. 4

    Return the pot to low heat and gently bring the mixture to 140°F (60°C) over 10-15 minutes to firm up the curds.

  5. 5

    Line a strainer with cheesecloth set over a large bowl, then pour in the mixture and let drain for 5-10 minutes.

  6. 6

    Rinse the curds under cold running water for about 30 seconds to remove any acidic taste, then drain again.

  7. 7

    Season the curds with salt and stir in cream if desired, then serve immediately or refrigerate.

Pro Tips for Perfect Homemade Cottage Cheese

  • Prep and measure every ingredient before you start cooking — it keeps the process smooth and stress-free.
  • Taste and adjust the seasoning right before serving; salt levels vary a lot between broth and cheese brands.
  • Use fresh garlic and herbs where the recipe calls for them — the flavor difference is noticeable.

Variations to Try

  • Swap the protein: use what you have on hand — chicken, beef, and shrimp all adapt well.
  • Dairy-free: use coconut cream or a dairy-free alternative.
  • Spice it up: add extra chili flakes for more heat.

Serving Suggestions

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 4 days.

Freezer

Cream-based sauces can separate when frozen. This dish is best eaten fresh or refrigerated. If freezing, store up to 2 months and stir well when reheating.

Reheat

Warm gently in a skillet over low heat, adding a splash of cream or broth to loosen the sauce. Avoid the microwave if possible — it can make the sauce break.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to make homemade cottage cheese?

About 45-60 minutes total. The active work is only 15-20 minutes — the rest is waiting for the milk to heat and curds to form.

Can I make cottage cheese with plant-based milk?

Traditional cottage cheese relies on casein protein in dairy milk. You can create a similar product with soy milk, but the result will differ in taste and texture.

What type of vinegar is best?

White distilled vinegar is the best all-around choice — neutral flavor that rinses away cleanly. Avoid balsamic or red wine vinegar.

How much cottage cheese does one gallon of milk make?

One gallon of whole milk typically yields 1.5-2 cups of curds (before adding cream). Whole milk produces more curds than skim milk.

Is homemade cottage cheese healthier than store-bought?

Nutritionally very similar. The key advantage of homemade is control — you eliminate preservatives and thickeners, and control sodium exactly. If you use organic milk, you get those benefits at a lower cost than buying organic cottage cheese.

Can I use ultra-pasteurized (UHT) milk for cottage cheese?

No — ultra-pasteurized milk won't curdle properly because the high heat alters its proteins so curds can't form. Stick with regular pasteurized whole milk.
