Ricotta Meatballs – Tender, Juicy, No Breadcrumbs

By Mars Published June 6, 2026
★★★★★ 4.7 (90 ratings)
📌 Save to Pinterest Share

Classic Italian meatballs rely on breadcrumbs and egg for moisture. Ricotta meatballs swap in whole-milk ricotta, and the result is a meatball so tender it practically melts — without a single breadcrumb. They’re naturally gluten-free, high-protein, and surprisingly forgiving for beginners.

This ricotta meatballs recipe uses Italian flavors and comes together with everyday ingredients — no special trip to a specialty store required.

Why You'll Love This Recipe

  • Adds moisture without softening the structure. The cheese’s curds hold water that releases slowly during cooking, keeping the meat juicy from edge to center.
  • Boosts protein and calcium. A half-cup of whole-milk ricotta adds ~14g protein and 250mg calcium.
  • Creates a pillowy texture. Where breadcrumbs can make meatballs dense, ricotta keeps them light.

Ricotta Meatballs

The juiciest ricotta meatballs you’ll ever make — tender inside, golden outside, no breadcrumbs required. Full recipe with make-ahead and freezer tips.

Prep 20 min
Cook 25 min
Total 45 min
Serves 4
Calories 320 kcal

Ingredients

4 servings

Instructions

  1. 1

    Drain the ricotta in a fine-mesh sieve for 10 minutes if wet.

  2. 2

    Gently combine ground beef, ricotta, egg, Parmigiano, garlic, parsley, salt, pepper, and oregano — do not overmix.

  3. 3

    Microwave a tiny piece for 20 seconds and taste; adjust seasoning.

  4. 4

    With damp hands, roll into 1.5-inch (4cm) balls, about 15-16 meatballs.

  5. 5

    Chill the shaped meatballs for 15 minutes.

  6. 6

    Pan-fry in olive oil 2-3 minutes per side until golden, then simmer in marinara 10 minutes to 160°F (71°C), or bake at 400°F (200°C) for 18-20 minutes.

  7. 7

    Rest 5 minutes before serving.

Pro Tips for Perfect Ricotta Meatballs

  • Don't overcrowd the pan when searing — work in batches so everything browns instead of steaming.
  • Keep the sauce at a gentle simmer, not a rolling boil — high heat can cause cream-based sauces to separate.
  • Don't skip the chilling step — it firms up the mixture so it holds together during cooking.
  • Let it rest for a few minutes before serving so the juices redistribute.

Variations to Try

  • Swap the protein: Absolutely. Lean poultry works beautifully with ricotta. Add 1 extra tbsp olive oil to keep them juicy. Cook to 165°F (74°C) internal temperature.
  • Oven / air-fryer method: Yes — bake at 400°F (200°C) for 18-20 minutes on a lined sheet, flipping once halfway. You'll lose a little of the pan-seared crust but gain a hands-off, less messy method.
  • Make-ahead tip: Yes — shape the meatballs and refrigerate them raw, covered, for up to 24 hours before cooking. The chill time actually firms them up and makes them easier to sear.

Serving Suggestions

Ricotta Meatballs pairs well with simple sides that let the main dish shine:

  • Warm tortillas or flatbread
  • Fluffy white rice
  • A crisp cucumber-tomato salad
  • Pickled onions or a tangy slaw

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 4 days.

Freezer

Freezes well in an airtight container for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before reheating.

Reheat

Reheat gently in a skillet over medium-low heat, or in the microwave in 30-second bursts, stirring in between.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are my ricotta meatballs falling apart?

Three likely causes: (1) the ricotta was too wet — drain it first; (2) you skipped the chill step; (3) you're flipping them too early — let them develop a crust before turning.

Can I use ground turkey or chicken instead of beef?

Absolutely. Lean poultry works beautifully with ricotta. Add 1 extra tbsp olive oil to keep them juicy. Cook to 165°F (74°C) internal temperature.

Can I bake these instead of pan-frying?

Yes — bake at 400°F (200°C) for 18-20 minutes on a lined sheet, flipping once halfway. You'll lose a little of the pan-seared crust but gain a hands-off, less messy method.

What's the best ricotta to use?

Whole-milk ricotta, well-drained in a fine mesh strainer for 15-20 minutes. Skim or part-skim ricotta carries too much water and will make the mixture loose.

Can I make the mixture ahead of time?

Yes — shape the meatballs and refrigerate them raw, covered, for up to 24 hours before cooking. The chill time actually firms them up and makes them easier to sear.

Do I need breadcrumbs at all?

No — that's the whole point of this recipe. The ricotta replaces the binding and moisture breadcrumbs normally provide, so the meatballs stay naturally gluten-free.
