Easy Chicken Tikka Masala Better Than Restaurant Takeout

★★★★★ 4.9 (387 reviews) · By Mars · February 10, 2026 · 40 min total
Share: 📌 Save to Pinterest 📄 Share

Chicken tikka masala is one of the most beloved dishes in the United States — creamy, aromatic, slightly sweet and tangy, deeply spiced without being overwhelmingly hot. It was reportedly the most popular restaurant dish in Britain for decades, and American dinner tables have caught on in a big way. The problem? Takeout tikka masala can run $18-22 a plate and varies wildly in quality. This version costs about $12 for four servings, takes 40 minutes, and I genuinely believe it tastes better than most delivery options.

The secret is a technique called spice blooming. Instead of adding your dry spices directly into liquid (where they stay harsh and flat), you toast them briefly in hot butter or oil before any liquid hits the pan. That 60 seconds of heat in fat activates the fat-soluble flavor compounds inside the spices — the same molecules that give garam masala its aromatic depth and cumin its earthy warmth. Bloom your spices properly and your kitchen will smell like the best Indian restaurant in town. Skip this step and your masala will taste dull and one-dimensional.

Why You'll Love This Chicken Tikka Masala

  • Deep, complex flavor in 40 minutes — The spice blooming technique builds restaurant-level aromatic depth without hours of simmering.
  • Charred chicken adds a smoky layer — Searing the marinated chicken until caramelized and slightly charred replicates the tandoor oven flavor that makes tikka masala special.
  • Adaptable heat level — This recipe is mild to medium as written. Add more cayenne or fresh chili for heat, or reduce the spices for a milder version your whole family will love.
  • Better the next day — Like all braised and sauced dishes, tikka masala improves overnight as the spices meld and deepen. This is the meal-prep MVP.
  • All spices from the regular grocery store — No specialty store required. Garam masala is now stocked at Walmart, Kroger, and every major grocery chain in the US.

Understanding the Spices (The American Guide)

If you're new to Indian-inspired cooking, the spice list might look intimidating. Here's a quick guide to what each one does and where to find it:

  • Garam masala — This is a blend (not a single spice) of warm aromatic spices: cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, cumin, coriander, black pepper. It's the heart of tikka masala. Find it in the spice aisle at any Walmart, Kroger, Target, or Whole Foods. McCormick and Frontier Co-Op both make good versions.
  • Cumin — Earthy, warm, slightly nutty. If you only have one spice on this list already, it's probably cumin.
  • Coriander — Ground from coriander seeds (the same plant as fresh cilantro, but completely different flavor). Bright, citrusy, and floral. Essential for that "Indian restaurant" background note.
  • Turmeric — Bright yellow, slightly earthy. Adds color and anti-inflammatory properties. Use sparingly — too much turns dishes bitter and stains everything orange.
  • Paprika — Used in the marinade for color and mild sweetness. Regular paprika works fine here (save the smoked paprika for our baked salmon recipe).

Kitchen Tools You'll Need

  • Large skillet or deep sauté pan (12-inch) — Wide enough to sear the chicken in a single layer. A 12-inch stainless steel or cast iron pan is ideal. The fond (browned bits) left from searing is pure flavor that you'll deglaze into the sauce.
  • Mixing bowl — For the yogurt marinade.
  • Grater or microplane — For the fresh ginger. Fresh ginger is significantly more aromatic than ground — don't substitute.
  • Wooden spoon or silicone spatula — For stirring the sauce and scraping up the fond.

Instructions

  1. Marinate the chicken: In a bowl, combine yogurt, lemon juice, garam masala, cumin, turmeric, paprika, and salt. Add chicken pieces and toss to coat. Marinate for at least 15 minutes at room temperature (or up to 8 hours in the fridge — longer = more flavorful). The yogurt tenderizes the chicken and helps it char beautifully.
  2. Char the chicken: Heat 1 tbsp oil in a large skillet over high heat until smoking. Add chicken pieces in a single layer, shaking off excess marinade. Sear without moving for 3-4 minutes until deeply caramelized and slightly charred on the bottom. Flip and cook 2 more minutes. The chicken doesn't need to be fully cooked yet — it will finish in the sauce. Transfer to a plate.
  3. Build the sauce base: Reduce heat to medium. Add butter to the same pan (don't wipe it — those browned bits are flavor). Once melted, add the diced onion. Cook, stirring occasionally, for 8-10 minutes until very soft and deeply golden. This step cannot be rushed — deeply caramelized onion is the backbone of the sauce.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, and bloom the spices: Add minced garlic and grated ginger. Cook 60 seconds, stirring. Add the dry spices (garam masala, cumin, coriander, turmeric, cayenne). Stir constantly for 60 seconds — you'll see the spices darken slightly and smell the aromatics bloom. This is the most important step: dry-toasting spices in fat activates their flavor compounds. Don't skip it and don't burn it.
  5. Simmer the tomato sauce: Pour in the crushed tomatoes. Stir well to combine with the spice-onion base, scraping up any fond from the bottom of the pan. Simmer over medium heat for 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens and the tomatoes lose their raw flavor. Season with salt and optional sugar.
  6. Add cream and finish the chicken: Pour in the heavy cream and stir to combine. Add the charred chicken pieces (plus any juices from the plate — liquid gold). Reduce heat to medium-low. Simmer for 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is fully cooked through and the sauce is rich, creamy, and coats the back of a spoon. Taste and adjust salt. Serve over basmati rice with fresh cilantro and warm naan.

🔑 Pro Tips for the Best Tikka Masala

  • Use ghee if you can find it. Ghee is clarified butter — butter with the milk solids and water removed. It has a higher smoke point than regular butter (which means it won't burn when searing) and a richer, nuttier flavor. Find it at Whole Foods, Trader Joe's, or the international foods aisle at most large grocery stores. It's worth it. But regular butter absolutely works.
  • Don't skip the marinade time. Even 15 minutes of yogurt marinating makes a significant difference in tenderness and the char you get when searing. If you have 8 hours (morning before a weeknight dinner), the result is even better.
  • Deeply caramelize the onion. 8-10 minutes is the minimum. You want the onion to be soft, translucent, and golden — not just softened and still raw-tasting. This step takes patience but creates the sweet, savory foundation that makes tikka masala taste complex.
  • The 60-second spice bloom is non-negotiable. You'll know it's working when the smell in your kitchen changes from raw spice powder to deeply aromatic and slightly toasty. Watch the heat carefully — you want to toast, not burn. If the spices start getting very dark, add a splash of water immediately.
  • Add a pinch of sugar at the end. Canned tomatoes are often quite acidic. A small pinch of sugar at the end of cooking balances this acidity and rounds out all the flavors without making the dish sweet. Taste and decide for yourself.

Variations & Substitutions

  • Chicken breast instead of thighs: Use boneless skinless breasts, cut smaller (1-inch pieces). Reduce the searing time to 2 minutes per side and the sauce simmering time to 7 minutes to avoid overcooking. Thighs are far more forgiving, but breast works if that's your preference.
  • Dairy-free version: Replace yogurt with full-fat coconut yogurt (Culina or So Delicious brands). Replace heavy cream with full-fat coconut cream. Use oil instead of butter. The flavor shifts slightly tropical but is still excellent.
  • Vegetarian version (Paneer Tikka Masala): Replace chicken with 14-16 oz of paneer (Indian fresh cheese — available at Whole Foods, Trader Joe's, and most Asian grocery stores), cubed and pan-fried until golden. Add to the sauce for the final 5 minutes. No marinade needed for paneer, though you can coat it in spices before frying.
  • Extra smoky: Add ¼ tsp smoked paprika to the sauce spices. Or — for true tandoor-smoky flavor — use the "coal smoking" technique: place a small piece of aluminum foil in the center of the finished curry, put a lit piece of charcoal on it, drizzle with a few drops of butter, then cover tightly for 2-3 minutes. Restaurant secret.
  • Slow cooker version: Sear the chicken and make the tomato sauce as directed (steps 1-5). Transfer to a slow cooker. Cook on low for 6-8 hours or high for 3-4 hours. Add cream in the last 30 minutes. Perfect for meal prep.

Serving Suggestions

  • Classic: Over fluffy basmati rice (rinse it well, cook 1:1.5 rice to water ratio). The rice soaks up the extra sauce and every bite is perfect.
  • With naan: Warm store-bought naan (Stonefire brand at Walmart/Kroger is excellent) or homemade naan for dipping and scooping. This is the most satisfying way to eat tikka masala.
  • Indian-inspired spread: Pair with a simple cucumber raita (yogurt + cucumber + cumin), sliced raw onion with a squeeze of lime, and mango chutney. This turns it into a restaurant-worthy spread.
  • Tikka masala pizza: Use leftover sauce as a pizza base, top with chicken pieces and mozzarella. Unconventional and absolutely delicious.

📦 Storage & Reheating

🧊 Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 4 days. The flavor deepens and improves significantly after the first day. Make a double batch specifically for leftovers.

❄️ Freezer

Freeze for up to 3 months. Tikka masala freezes exceptionally well — one of the best freezer meals there is. Freeze in individual portions for fast lunches. Cream-based sauces can separate on reheating; stir well while warming.

♨️ Reheating

Reheat in a saucepan over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally. Add a splash of water or cream if the sauce has thickened too much. Microwave works well too — cover and heat in 90-second bursts, stirring between each.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is garam masala and where do I find it?

Garam masala is a ground spice blend used throughout South Asian cooking. The exact blend varies by brand and region, but typically includes cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, cumin, coriander, black pepper, and sometimes nutmeg. It's warm and aromatic rather than spicy-hot. You can now find it at virtually every US grocery store — McCormick and Frontier Co-Op sell it in the regular spice aisle at Walmart, Kroger, and Target. Whole Foods carries several specialty brands. No specialty store trip required.

Can I make this less spicy for kids?

Yes — this recipe is designed to be mild-to-medium as written. To make it truly mild: omit the cayenne entirely. Reduce the garam masala by half. The dish will still be aromatic and flavorful but will have zero heat. Kids generally love tikka masala because of its creamy, slightly sweet tomato flavor — without the spice heat it's very approachable for picky eaters.

What's the difference between tikka masala and butter chicken?

They're closely related but not identical. Butter chicken (murgh makhani) typically has a milder, sweeter, butterier sauce and uses chicken that was already cooked in a tandoor. Tikka masala is slightly more tomato-forward and spicier, and the "tikka" refers to chicken that's been marinated and charred. In practice, many restaurant versions blur together. This recipe leans toward the tikka masala side with the charred chicken and more forward spicing.

Can I use Greek yogurt for the marinade?

Yes — plain Greek yogurt works perfectly. It's actually better than regular yogurt in some ways because it's thicker and coats the chicken more generously. Make sure it's plain (no flavoring) and whole-milk for the richest result. Low-fat yogurt tends to separate and doesn't adhere as well to the chicken during searing.

Why does my sauce taste too acidic?

Canned tomatoes vary significantly in acidity — some brands are quite sharp. Two solutions: (1) Add ½-1 tsp of sugar to balance the acidity without adding sweetness. (2) Let the tomato sauce simmer longer (15 minutes instead of 10) to cook off more of the volatile acids. Adding the cream also helps balance acidity. In a pinch, a tiny pinch of baking soda neutralizes acidity chemically — use very sparingly (a literal pinch).
