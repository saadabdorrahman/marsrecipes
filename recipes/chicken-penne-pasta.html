Chicken Penne Pasta – One Skillet, 30 Minutes

By Mars Published July 6, 2026
★★★★★ 4.9 (80 ratings)
📌 Save to Pinterest Share

This chicken penne pasta recipe is the weeknight dinner I make when everyone is hungry and nobody wants to wait: juicy golden chicken bites and al dente penne tossed in a garlic-parmesan cream sauce, all in one skillet in about 30 minutes. No fancy technique, no obscure ingredients — just the kind of creamy chicken and penne pasta that gets quiet-table approval every single time.

This chicken penne pasta recipe uses Italian-American flavors and comes together with everyday ingredients — no special trip to a specialty store required.

Why You'll Love This Recipe

  • One skillet, one pot. The chicken sears and the sauce builds in the same pan, so the browned bits from the chicken become flavor in the sauce.
  • The sauce clings. Penne’s ridges and tube shape trap the garlic-parmesan cream, so every bite carries sauce instead of leaving it at the bottom of the bowl.
  • Real 30 minutes. The pasta boils while the chicken cooks — the timeline overlaps on purpose, exactly like our cajun chicken pasta.

Ingredients You'll Need

Chicken breast keeps it lean, but boneless thighs are even more forgiving and stay juicier if your stove runs hot. Heavy cream makes the sauce silky without any flour; if you only have half-and-half, see the FAQ below for the adjustment. Parmesan should be grated fresh off the block — pre-shredded cheese is coated in anti-caking starch and melts grainy. Italian seasoning and smoked paprika season the chicken crust; swap in Cajun seasoning if you like heat. A handful of baby spinach or halved cherry tomatoes stirred in at the end adds color and freshness without extra work.

Chicken Penne Pasta

This creamy chicken penne pasta recipe is ready in 30 minutes in one skillet — juicy seared chicken and penne in a garlic-parmesan cream sauce the whole family will love.

Prep 10 min
Cook 20 min
Total 30 min
Serves 4
Calories 640 kcal
📌 Save to Pinterest

Ingredients

4 servings

Instructions

  1. 1

    Boil the penne in well-salted water for 1 minute less than the package says; reserve 1 cup of pasta water before draining.

  2. 2

    Toss the chicken pieces with Italian seasoning, smoked paprika, ½ tsp salt, and the pepper.

  3. 3

    Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high; sear the chicken in a single layer 3 minutes without moving, then flip and cook 3-4 minutes more to 165°F (74°C). Transfer to a plate.

  4. 4

    Lower the heat to medium; melt the butter and cook the garlic for 30 seconds until fragrant.

  5. 5

    Pour in the heavy cream, scraping up the browned bits, and simmer 2-3 minutes until slightly thickened.

  6. 6

    Remove from the heat and stir in the parmesan a handful at a time until smooth.

  7. 7

    Add the drained penne, chicken, and a splash of pasta water; toss until the sauce coats everything, season to taste, and finish with parsley.

Pro Tips for Perfect Chicken Penne Pasta

  • Don't overcrowd the pan when searing — work in batches so everything browns instead of steaming.
  • Keep the sauce at a gentle simmer, not a rolling boil — high heat can cause cream-based sauces to separate.
  • Watch the garlic closely once it hits the pan — it goes from fragrant to bitter in seconds.
  • Use a meat thermometer to confirm doneness rather than guessing by eye — it's the easiest way to avoid over- or undercooking.

Variations to Try

  • Swap the protein: You can cook the chicken and sauce up to 2 days ahead and refrigerate them separately from the pasta. Reheat gently with a splash of milk or broth, then toss with freshly cooked penne for the best texture.
  • Ingredient swap: Half-and-half plus one teaspoon of flour cooked with the garlic gives a lighter but still silky sauce. Whole milk alone will be thinner — simmer it a few extra minutes and add the parmesan off the heat.
  • Swap the protein: shrimp or firm tofu both work well in place of chicken with a shorter cook time.

Serving Suggestions

Garlic bread for the sauce, and something crisp to cut the richness — an arugula salad with lemon vinaigrette or simple roasted broccoli. If your table loves creamy chicken pasta nights, put our crockpot marry me chicken pasta on next week’s menu.

  • Steamed rice or cauliflower rice
  • A simple green salad with vinaigrette
  • Roasted or steamed vegetables
  • Crusty bread for the sauce

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 3–4 days.

Freezer

Cream-based sauces can separate when frozen. This dish is best eaten fresh or refrigerated. If freezing, store up to 2 months and stir well when reheating.

Reheat

Warm gently in a skillet over low heat, adding a splash of cream or broth to loosen the sauce. Avoid the microwave if possible — it can make the sauce break.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a different pasta shape?

Yes — rigatoni, ziti, or fusilli all work well because they hold the creamy sauce in their ridges. Adjust the cooking time to the package directions and keep the pasta al dente, since it finishes in the hot sauce.

How do I keep the chicken from drying out?

Cut the breast into even 1-inch pieces, don't crowd the pan, and pull the chicken off the heat as soon as it reaches 165°F (74°C). It rests while you build the sauce and finishes warming through at the end.

Can I make chicken penne pasta ahead of time?

You can cook the chicken and sauce up to 2 days ahead and refrigerate them separately from the pasta. Reheat gently with a splash of milk or broth, then toss with freshly cooked penne for the best texture.

What can I substitute for heavy cream?

Half-and-half plus one teaspoon of flour cooked with the garlic gives a lighter but still silky sauce. Whole milk alone will be thinner — simmer it a few extra minutes and add the parmesan off the heat.

Can I use rotisserie chicken?

Absolutely. Skip the searing step, shred about 3 cups of rotisserie chicken, and stir it in during the last 2–3 minutes so it heats through without drying out.

Why is my cream sauce grainy?

The pan was too hot when the parmesan went in. Always pull the skillet off direct heat, let the sauce stop bubbling, then stir in finely grated parmesan a handful at a time.
