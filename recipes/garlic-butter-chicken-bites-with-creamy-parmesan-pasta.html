Ingredients You'll Need

Everything here is available at any major supermarket. A few notes on what makes each ingredient work:

  • Chicken breasts: Cut into 1-inch pieces so they cook fast and stay juicy. Thighs are even more forgiving
  • Butter: The real deal, unsalted. This is a butter-forward dish — don’t substitute
  • Fresh garlic: 5 cloves for the chicken, 4 for the sauce. Fresh only — garlic powder won’t give you the same punch
  • Heavy cream: The base of the sauce. Don’t sub with milk or half-and-half unless you want a much thinner result
  • Fresh parmesan: Grate it yourself from a block. Pre-grated cheese has anti-caking agents that make the sauce grainy
  • Pasta: Penne, rigatoni, or fettuccine all work great. Even spaghetti or linguine
  • Don’t crowd the chicken: Cook in batches if needed. Crowding creates steam instead of a golden sear — the #1 mistake
  • Use fresh parmesan: The difference between smooth and grainy sauce is almost always the parmesan
  • Save your pasta water: Scoop out ½ cup before draining — it’s the perfect sauce thinner
  • Cook pasta al dente: It finishes cooking in the sauce. Fully cooked pasta = mushy pasta
  • Rest the chicken: A quick 2-minute rest after cooking keeps the juices inside the bites

Garlic Butter Chicken Bites

These garlic butter chicken bites with creamy parmesan pasta come together in one skillet in just 35 minutes. Juicy golden chicken pieces tossed in a rich, garlicky parmesan cream sauce — a weeknight dinner the whole family will love.

Prep 10 min
Cook 25 min
Total 35 min
Serves 4
Calories 650 kcal
📌 Save to Pinterest

Ingredients

4 servings
  • For The Chicken Bites
  • For The Creamy Parmesan Pasta

Instructions

  1. 1

    Cook the pasta: Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook pasta until al dente. Reserve 1/2 cup pasta water, then drain and set aside.

  2. 2

    Season the chicken: Pat chicken pieces dry and season with Italian seasoning, paprika, salt, and pepper.

  3. 3

    Sear the chicken: Melt 2 tbsp butter in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add chicken in a single layer (work in batches). Cook 3-4 minutes undisturbed, then flip and cook 2-3 more minutes until golden and cooked through (165°F). Remove to a plate.

  4. 4

    Build the garlic base: In the same skillet, melt remaining 1 tbsp butter over medium heat. Add garlic and cook 30 seconds until fragrant.

  5. 5

    Deglaze: Pour in chicken broth and scrape up the browned bits. Let reduce by half, about 2 minutes.

  6. 6

    Make the cream sauce: Add heavy cream and Italian seasoning. Simmer 3-4 minutes until slightly thickened.

  7. 7

    Add parmesan: Remove from heat and stir in grated parmesan until melted. Add pasta water if needed.

  8. 8

    Toss pasta: Add cooked pasta to the sauce and toss to coat.

  9. 9

    Serve: Add chicken bites back to the pan. Garnish with parsley and extra parmesan. Serve immediately.

Pro Tips for Perfect Garlic Butter Chicken Bites

  • Don't overcrowd the pan when searing — work in batches so everything browns instead of steaming.
  • Keep the sauce at a gentle simmer, not a rolling boil — high heat can cause cream-based sauces to separate.
  • Watch the garlic closely once it hits the pan — it goes from fragrant to bitter in seconds.
  • Use a meat thermometer to confirm doneness rather than guessing by eye — it's the easiest way to avoid over- or undercooking.

Variations to Try

  • Swap the protein: Absolutely — boneless skinless thighs are actually better here. They’re juicier and more forgiving if slightly overcooked. Use the same method.
  • Make-ahead tip: Best made fresh. You can cook and slice the chicken up to 2 days ahead. The sauce comes together in minutes once you start cooking, so active time is minimal.
  • Ingredient swap: Half-and-half will work for a lighter sauce but won’t be as thick or rich. Avoid milk — it’s too thin and may curdle when you add the parmesan.

Serving Suggestions

This dish is hearty enough to stand alone, but a simple side rounds it out perfectly:

  • Steamed rice or cauliflower rice
  • A simple green salad with vinaigrette
  • Roasted or steamed vegetables
  • Crusty bread for the sauce

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 3–4 days.

Freezer

Cream-based sauces can separate when frozen. This dish is best eaten fresh or refrigerated. If freezing, store up to 2 months and stir well when reheating.

Reheat

Warm gently in a skillet over low heat, adding a splash of cream or broth to loosen the sauce. Avoid the microwave if possible — it can make the sauce break.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use chicken thighs instead of breasts?

Absolutely — boneless skinless thighs are actually better here. They’re juicier and more forgiving if slightly overcooked. Use the same method.

Can I make this ahead of time?

Best made fresh. You can cook and slice the chicken up to 2 days ahead. The sauce comes together in minutes once you start cooking, so active time is minimal.

Can I substitute the heavy cream?

Half-and-half will work for a lighter sauce but won’t be as thick or rich. Avoid milk — it’s too thin and may curdle when you add the parmesan.

My sauce is too thick — how do I fix it?

Add the reserved pasta water one tablespoon at a time, stirring until you reach the right consistency. The starch helps the sauce cling without making it watery.

Can I freeze this dish?

We don’t recommend it — cream-based pasta sauces tend to separate and become grainy when thawed. Make a fresh batch instead — it only takes 35 minutes!
